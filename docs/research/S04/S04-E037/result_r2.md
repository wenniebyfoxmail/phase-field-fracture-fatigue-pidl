# R2 result — finite first trial, but no accepted physical substep

Run `S04-E037-R003` used commit `0997e32a0b68ca3d8546a4ccce7f1d4d2094185d` on Taobo GPU5. It reused the completed R001 original arm and executed only the repaired new arm. Execution ended normally in 88 seconds; raw result retrieval is verified. This is a negative result for this repaired original-NN primal-dual candidate reaching an accepted first physical substep, not a conclusion about hard irreversibility formulations in general.

The repair removed the R002 immediate energy-domain failure: the c1/s1 trial reached finite extended physical energy `E_el + E_d = 0.0055380812`, with original-definition diagnostic `E0 = 0.0055275704`. It ran 1,320 primal-gradient updates, 1,551 actual-merit evaluations, and eight multiplier outer iterations. However, it stopped at the declared outer/update cap without committing history: `max(h-d)=3.526e-4` versus `5e-6`, `d_min=-2.398e-4`, scaled complementarity `3.519e-4` versus `5e-5`, and parameter-Lagrangian RMS `3.723e-4` versus `5e-5`. The multiplier penalty reached `rho=1e6`; the remaining limitation is feasibility/stationarity of the NN-parameter solve, not the earlier log-domain crash. Its raw retreat is about 11.7 times smaller than R001 c1/s1 (`4.141e-3`), but still about 70.5 times above the required hard-constraint tolerance.

The saved c1/s1 field is an explicitly unaccepted diagnostic trial. Relative to the original arm at the same c1/s1 phase, it has strain-norm relative L2 `7.86%` (area mean ratio `1.00046`), raw psi relative L2 `15.64%` (ratio `0.87059`), and trial fatigue-A relative L2 `7.16%` (ratio `1.01471`). Since no history was committed and neither c1 peak nor cycle-end was reached, these values do not establish FEM improvement, fatigue-evolution improvement, or a viable trajectory.

The R2 first-step cost was `82.94s`; original c1/s1 took `20.51s`. This is not a speed comparison at equal acceptance: R2 used 1,320 updates plus dual/check overhead and still did not accept the state. No speedup is claimed.

The physical conclusion is narrow but useful: extending AT1 outside the feasible domain and fixing the exact `h==1` nodes makes the objective evaluable, yet a finite PHR/RPROP parameter solve still lets many free nodes retreat below the previous damage lower bound. A subsequent design would need feasibility built into the damage parameterization or an explicit projected constrained subsolve; that would be a new method change and requires a new prospectively declared run.
