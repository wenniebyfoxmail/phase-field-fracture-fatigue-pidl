## Result

Changing the aligned FEM reference from standard cadence to `n_step=10` does
not remove the PIDL mechanism gap.

At c69:

| Reference | damage tip2 | alpha_bar tip2 | alpha_bar p99 | raw psi tip2 | active psi tip2 | Delta E_d |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| standard FEM | 1.012 | 0.466 | 0.191 | 1.330 | 0.0287 | 0.2505 |
| n_step10 FEM | 1.008 | 0.373 | 0.202 | 1.583 | 0.0233 | 0.2500 |

The numbers are PIDL/FEM ratios on common probes.  Damage location/amount near
the tip is essentially aligned, but history and active/degraded driver are not.
The raw tensile driver can be comparable or even larger in PIDL, while the
active driver collapses after degradation.  This is the same mechanism
interpretation as before:

```text
PIDL forms damage in roughly the right place,
but the degraded active work feeding fatigue history is too small near the tip.
Then alpha_bar and incremental E_d fall behind FEM as cycles accumulate.
```

for elastic-energy conclusions without a refreshed FEM energy export.

field is not too weak; the degraded active work is almost extinguished around
the near-tip probes after damage saturation.

## Decision

The remaining gap is not mainly a FEM substep-count/reference-choice artifact,
nor a fixed-cycle-only artefact.  Using n_step10 as the FEM reference gives the
same conclusion as standard FEM, and using PIDL event states makes the active
driver gap sharper.  The next useful PIDL experiments should target the local
active-driver/history feedback, not another FEM cadence alignment.

The staged-head implementation is a controlled optimizer-path intervention,

one final output layer; the "uv head" and "alpha head" are the corresponding
rows of that output layer.  Therefore this test can strengthen/slacken the
output-head stages with separate optimizers or learning rates, but it is not a
true independently learned trunk for displacement and damage.

`trunk_shared`, `uv_head`, `alpha_head`).  This is needed to distinguish
whether a candidate is solving elastic relaxation through the displacement
rows or mostly changing the damage row to reduce the coupled energy.
